#Don’t Join – Author’s Reflection
The goal of Don't Join was to tell a story from the perspective of Droids being forced to follow their programming in the Star Wars universe, to explore the horror of being trapped in a cycle of being forced to work with no escape.
Given the time limitations and my own lack of experience in writing, both in general and in writing an interactive narrative, many ideas that I had were cut down in an attempt to give a realistic scope that I was able to achieve in time for the due date.
In the future an earlier outline, with more early feedback would lead to a more consistent scope and writing quality. I enjoyed the challenge I gave myself in trying to have a branching path at the start exploring ways of describing the same interaction with and without audio descriptions and while I originally intended it to have longer effects on the overall narrative.
I'm glad I cut the sound restriction concept off in that first act to streamline future interactions not needing to also account for the player being deaf or blind, as originally the ability to see was also a part of the creation introduction but was cut early for scope, complexity, and my own skill in writing.
Looking back, I wish I had made a better structure in advance to work off of, an early outline and perhaps done drawings for myself for the layout of the ship so I had a better picture in my head to relay to the reader and a stronger goal in mind.
I think I got the feeling of being trapped in a mundane cycle of being forced to follow orders, while allowing for exploration and breaking out of the cycle.
Thanks to discussing my story and feedback with another student I was also recommended The Murderbot Diaries by Matha Wells. While I have not read it yet, their description of the story and feelings evoked by both Murderbot and my game seemed to align in their mind and I'm excited to further explore the concept of freedom and autonomy, which was the goal and aim for my own story.
While I did use Star Wars as an initial setting, I didn’t expand out the descriptions there to be enjoyable for people not already familiar with the setting, leaving newcomers feeling confused and left out due to a lot of assumptions being made around prior knowledge of the setting. I didn’t make this a priority due to the time and word constraints I think it would have added to the experience to make it more accessible without assumptions around things like “what a droid is” or “what this specific kind of droid is”.
Overall, I’m proud of what I managed in the time given and I would love to take a second run at the same theme of story later with more experience.
